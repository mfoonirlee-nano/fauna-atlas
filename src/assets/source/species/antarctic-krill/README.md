## Shared morphology and evidence boundary

Adult frames use a mostly transparent body with scattered red pigment, large dark compound eyes, long sensory flagella, a segmented abdomen and a terminal fan formed by the uropods and telson. Fine thoracic appendages form the feeding basket, and five paired pleopods occur below the abdomen. The prompts retain exposed feathery thoracic gills and reject the heavy claws, opaque orange shell and long toothed rostrum of a generic cooked prawn or lobster. The generated pixels cannot replace a specimen key or prove every appendage count.

A green digestive tract can reflect recently ingested pigmented food. It is not a fixed body colour or proof of a strictly herbivorous diet. Any visible photophores remain small anatomical structures rather than bright decorative lights.

Frame 01 places an adult below sea ice as one seasonal representative setting. Antarctic Krill also occupies broad circumpolar pelagic waters, including oceanic water over deep basins, so the cover does not make the species an ice-edge or continental-shelf specialist. Frame 03 shows one representative late larval form at the ice underside; it is not a complete developmental series or a diagnostic stage plate.

Frame 04 reconstructs one plausible dawn depth transition. Diel vertical migration changes with region, season, sea ice, developmental stage, light and predation pressure, and some surveys find no consistent dawn or dusk displacement. Frame 05 shows a Chinstrap Penguin approaching a swarm without contact. It provides no capture result, diet fraction, local density or predator-response estimate.

Frame 06 shows a representative rectangular scientific midwater net. Its geometry resembles research systems used to sample krill, but the image does not document an exact RMT configuration, deployment protocol, vessel or survey. Net samples can describe body length and developmental composition; acoustic and net methods retain different detection and sampling biases.

## Runtime conversion

The six source PNGs were compressed in place with the repository's TinyPNG workflow, then converted with the documented runtime settings:

```bash
npm run compress -- src/assets/source/species/antarctic-krill/*.png
npm run convert:webp -- src/assets/source/species/antarctic-krill/*-source.png
```

The converter uses `cwebp -q 82 -m 6 -mt` and removes `-source` from each runtime filename.
