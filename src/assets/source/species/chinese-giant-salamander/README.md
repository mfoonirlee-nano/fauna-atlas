The image set follows the evidence boundaries in [`docs/research/chinese-giant-salamander-profile.md`](../../../../../docs/research/chinese-giant-salamander-profile.md). Historical records under the name `Andrias davidianus` include multiple lineages now split or still under study, and modern translocation has further mixed geography and ancestry. The images therefore depict a scientifically plausible representative animal without claiming a genetically verified lineage or exact locality. Adults have broad flattened heads, very small eyes, rough folded skin, short limbs and one compressed tail; they do not carry the permanent feathery gills of an axolotl. Frame 04 shows ecological proximity rather than a witnessed predation event. Frame 05 uses the documented male-guarding context but does not claim sex can be identified from appearance alone. Frame 06 shows water sampling only and does not expose a detection point or imply that eDNA alone proves population recovery.
