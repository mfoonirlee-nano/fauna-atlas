- The cover uses a complete adult with eight walking legs and two pedipalps. The close view crops through walking legs on purpose and makes no complete appendage-count claim.
- The foraging frame shows approach toward one orthopteran. It does not prove capture, a preferred prey, or a fixed diet. No bird appears because the common name does not describe the usual diet.
- The defensive frame shows a threat posture. A still image cannot prove that the spider produced sound, released urticating setae, or injected venom.
- The egg-sac frame contains one sealed sac. It does not encode clutch size, development stage, or hatching success.
- The survey frame shows a plausible non-invasive method. It does not represent a real team, locality, abundance estimate, or IUCN assessment.
