Adult frames show a shell-free terrestrial hermit crab with a broad cephalothorax, hardened dorsal abdominal plates, one pair of unequal heavy chelae, three prominent pairs of walking legs, reduced posterior appendages, stalked eyes and two antennal pairs. Natural colour varies across and within populations, so the blue-violet, red-brown and cream animals in this set represent plausible morphs rather than fixed sex, age or locality markers.

Frame 02 shows a representative early juvenile carrying a gastropod shell during the transition from the marine glaucothoe stage to terrestrial life. Generated pixels cannot establish the animal's species, age or exact developmental stage. Adults abandon shells after the abdomen hardens.

Frame 03 reconstructs a female at the surf with an attached egg mass. The adult stays on rock with only the rear underside in the wash. The image does not prove hatching time, larval number, tide phase or swimming ability; adults rely on air-breathing branchiostegal lungs and do not live underwater.

Frame 04 starts with a partly opened coconut. Large individuals can open coconuts through a prolonged, multi-step process. Coconut forms one part of a broad diet that also includes fruits, seeds, nuts, palm pith, carrion and occasional live prey.

Frame 05 shows antennal sampling beside ripe palm fruit. Experiments demonstrate sensitive aerial olfaction and identify acetoin as one useful food odor, but a still image cannot show detection distance, chemical identity or a causal behavioral response.

Frame 06 reconstructs non-contact night documentation on a forest road. Carapace groove photographs can support individual identification, while road counts retain effects from weather, season, detectability and observer effort. The scene does not depict a real survey team or protocol.
