The set covers a forest-canopy hero, adult sex comparison, sealed-cavity feeding, canopy fruit-taking, full-wing flight and community nest monitoring. Each frame reconstructs a general natural-history scene. None records a named bird, locality, nest, project or measured behavior.

The session did not retain a byte-for-byte prompt transcript in the repository, and the compressed PNGs contain no prompt metadata. The blocks below are normalized records of the generation constraints and accepted pixels. Generator output IDs, rejection reasons, dimensions and hashes remain independently checkable.

## Scientific and editorial boundaries

Source labels resolve in the [Great Hornbill research brief](../../../../../docs/research/great-hornbill-profile.md).

- Shared morphology follows the IUCN assessment and IUCN SSC Hornbill Specialist Group: a huge yellow bill; a low yellow casque; black body; pale neck often stained yellow by preen oil; white wing band; and white tail crossed by a broad black subterminal band. Adult males have a red iris, black orbital skin and a larger black-edged casque. Adult females have a white to blue-white iris, red or pink orbital skin and a smaller casque without the male-type black rim. [S02][S08]
- Frame 01 supports the complete male-coded silhouette and right-weighted hero composition. Distance and lossy conversion leave its iris unreadable, and the side view does not teach the casque's full forked geometry.
- Frame 02 supports an adult sex comparison through the left bird's deep red iris, black orbital skin and larger black-edged casque, and the right bird's pale iris, red-pink orbital skin and smaller unlined casque. The generated pair is not a specimen, calibrated color chart, measurement sample or record of two wild individuals. [S08]
- Frame 03 shows an outside male-coded red iris, an inside female-coded pale iris, a narrow feeding slit, one fruit and a rough organic-looking seal. Published summaries do not establish a universal seal recipe or ingredient ratio, so product copy must not name fixed proportions of feces, mud, pulp or plant fragments. [S02][S07][S12]
- Frame 04 shows one red-eyed male-coded bird during a fruit-taking instant. The branch represents a generic small fleshy-fruited tree; the image cannot identify a fig species, prove swallowing or measure seed dispersal. [S02][S07]
- Frame 05 shows complete broad wings, separated primary feathers, a continuous white wing band and the white tail's broad black band. A still image cannot establish speed, altitude, route, migration or audible wingbeat. BirdLife classifies the species as non-migratory. [S03][S08][S12]
- Frame 06 generalizes community nest monitoring described in Thailand and India. It cannot identify a project, community, country, safe approach distance, occupied nest, intervention method or breeding outcome. [S02]
- No accepted frame establishes body length, mass, wingspan, abundance, age beyond adult-coded plumage, individual identity, subspecies or conservation status.
- No accepted frame contains visible text, labels, arrows, maps, scale bars, cages, feeders, branding or watermarks.
