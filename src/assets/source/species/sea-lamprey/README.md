## Evidence and depiction boundaries

- Adult frames show a jawless, scaleless animal with no paired fins. A lateral view exposes seven round pores on the near side, not all fourteen branchial pores.
- The two low dorsal fins, broad spade-shaped tail, single median nostril and circular oral disc form a visual anchor. Generated anatomy cannot replace a specimen diagnosis.
- Frame 01 represents generic cool coastal water. The high dorsal view hides the branchial row and confines the S-curved animal to the right; it cannot identify a locality, anadromous or landlocked origin, sex, age or measured length.
- Frame 02 makes the oral disc readable without treating it as horror imagery. Its keratin toothlet arrangement is illustrative, not a voucher-level count or diagnosis.
- Frame 03 reconstructs one stone-moving act by a sex-indeterminate breeding adult. It does not prove a completed nest, mating, eggs, spawning success or parental care.
- Frame 04 is accepted only in the management context of the introduced populations in Lake Erie, Michigan, Huron and Superior, not disputed Lake Ontario. The hidden contact site carries no visible wound or blood; one attachment does not establish host death or population impact. Sea lamprey remains native across its Atlantic range.
- Frame 05 shows a toothless larval oral hood and concealed eye at a soft-sediment surface. Fine sediment covers the branchial row, so the image supplies no pore count; larval external appearance alone cannot establish species identity.
- Frame 06 illustrates sample collection only. One water sample cannot establish presence, absence, abundance, invasion status or population trend; a validated species-specific laboratory assay supplies identification.
- No frame supplies a distribution record, abundance estimate, management outcome or real protocol. No frame contains readable text, a logo or a watermark.
