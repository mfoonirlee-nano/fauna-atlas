## Evidence boundaries

- The morphology anchor follows *Dugong dugon*: a rounded head, downturned bristled muzzle, no external ear pinnae or dorsal fin, two foreflippers, no hind limbs and a horizontal crescent-shaped tail fluke. A generated image cannot diagnose an individual or confirm provenance.
- Image 02 illustrates one plausible feeding action. A feeding trail does not identify the animal, establish a daily ration or estimate local abundance.
- Image 03 uses a generalized shallow tropical seagrass mosaic. It does not map a named meadow, depth contour or current range boundary.
- Image 04 shows a brief surface breath, not a fixed dive interval. Dugongs expose paired nostrils but do not produce the tall visible blow associated with large whales.
- Image 05 follows the usual single-calf pattern. The frame cannot establish age, sex, pregnancy interval, nursing status or kinship from appearance alone.
- Image 06 illustrates water collection for environmental DNA. One sample can support a recent-detection inference after controls and laboratory confirmation; it cannot count animals or prove a breeding population.
- The frames avoid a fixed annual migration route. Tracking studies show strong movement variation among individuals, including occasional long-distance movement.
