## Shared morphology and evidence boundary

The adult frame uses one pale domesticated male so its paired pectinate antennae remain readable. The resting forewings overlap much of the hindwings, as they can in a live moth; the frame does not turn reduced flight performance into winglessness. Pale colour, body size, markings, cocoon colour and life-history timing vary among breeds.

The feeding and spinning frames use a common pale fifth-instar form. They preserve a brown head, three thoracic leg pairs, abdominal prolegs and a modest rear horn. Frame 04 shows an early loose silk scaffold while the larva remains visible. It does not expose a finished cocoon or claim a filament diameter, length or yield.

Frame 03 represents one hatch beside developed eggs. Egg colour changes with strain, embryonic state and diapause, so the slate-grey cluster is not a universal colour chart. Frame 05 uses a deliberately opened observation window to reveal one intact pupa. A natural sealed cocoon would hide this stage.

Frame 06 reconstructs routine captive care in an unnamed facility. Separate trays suggest line-by-line management, but the scene does not reproduce a gene bank, country, accession system, feeding schedule, disease-control method or measured outcome.

## Runtime conversion
