## Evidence boundaries

- A stocky body, dense brown coat, small ears and eyes, large hind feet and a broad flattened scaly tail anchor the reconstruction to a beaver. Eurasian and North American beavers remain too similar for reliable identification from an ordinary photograph; the species identity comes from the controlled asset brief and provenance.
- Image 01 supports visible coat, body and tail descriptions. It does not establish sex, age beyond a broad adult reconstruction, locality or population membership.
- Image 02 shows one large near-side hind foot and the whole tail. Some toes overlap, so product copy must not claim that all five digits can be counted.
- Image 03 depicts one dammed small waterway. It cannot prove that all Eurasian beavers build dams or that this site reduced floods, improved water quality or increased species richness.
- Image 04 shows active contact with a rooted deciduous stem and a small exposed orange incisor. The plant cannot be identified reliably, and the still image cannot separate feeding from cutting material for transport or construction.
- Image 05 shows one adult beside two kits. It does not establish the adult's sex, biological parentage, kit ages, litter size for the species or mating system.
- Image 06 reconstructs non-contact water-level monitoring. One visit, one reading and one animal cannot establish abundance, population trend or a hydrological effect.
