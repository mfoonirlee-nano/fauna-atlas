## Evidence boundaries

- Adult anchors are a broad shallow translucent saucer bell, four horseshoe-shaped gonads, four short frilled oral arms, branching canals and a dense fringe of short fine marginal tentacles. Long sea-nettle streamers, a box-shaped bell and a fried-egg center are excluded.
- The four gonads are a familiar *Aurelia* clue, not a species-level diagnosis. The alt text and captions call the images generated reconstructions, and the profile requires locality, vouchers and molecular evidence for reliable identification.
- The habitat frame is a generalized North Atlantic embayment, not a named occurrence site. Exactly three separated animals prevent a scenic aggregation from becoming an unsupported abundance claim.
- The feeding frame treats copepods as sparse millimeter-scale prey and shows interception at the bell margin and oral arms. It does not depict active pursuit, giant prey, a fixed diet or a measured clearance rate.
- The life-cycle frame is a developmental reconstruction at millimeter scale. The first result reversed the strobila attachment and was rejected; a targeted edit attached its basal stalk to the overhead substrate, placed the disc stack distally, and moved one eight-rayed ephyra below it. The accepted output contains six visible discs, within the variable multi-disc form, despite the corrective prompt using seven as a generation control.
- The monitoring frame combines visual imaging, a sensor package, a surface water sample and field notes without handling animals. It does not imply that one eDNA sample identifies species, estimates abundance or establishes a global trend.
- The images avoid claims that local aggregations prove global increase, that IUCN NE means Least Concern, or that visible Chinese and Pacific historical records necessarily represent *A. aurita* sensu stricto.
