## Evidence and depiction boundaries

- The animal is a Goliath frog, not an African bullfrog, cane toad, horned frog or American bullfrog. Every close frame keeps the olive-brown mottled back, short dorsal ridges, broad flat head, a small flat tympanum no larger than the visible eye, barred powerful hind limbs and yellow-orange ventral surfaces.
- The images communicate a very large frog without placing a hand, ruler or familiar object beside it. They do not establish a measured length, mass, sex or age for any depicted individual.
- Frame 03 represents connected river and riparian habitat below the species' published upper elevation. It does not identify a river, protected area, flow rate, water-quality measurement or occupied site.
- Frame 04 reconstructs the 2022 field study's observation that adults forage on land at night beyond the river bank. It shows no prey and cannot identify diet, distance travelled or feeding success.
- Frame 05 resembles the study's shallow type-3 gravel nest. Researchers documented cleared pools, displaced stones, eggs and tadpoles, and photographed an adult at a nest at night. The image does not prove which frog made the pool, moved a particular stone, guarded a clutch or produced offspring.
- Frame 06 represents a visual encounter survey. It does not reproduce a real protocol, person, site, abundance estimate or result.
- No frame shows handling, capture, bait, traps, exposed eggs, a frog race, readable field notes, a logo or a population count.

## Anatomical correction passes

Static review found that the first versions of frames 01, 02, 04, 05 and 06 overstated the round tympanum, which made the frogs resemble bullfrogs. Those five sources received localized imagegen edits before compression and conversion. Frame 03 remained unchanged because the animal is a small part of the habitat view.

The accepted correction instruction was scene-specific but kept one anatomical target: replace any eye-sized circular ring behind the eye with a low-contrast flat membrane about half the visible eye diameter, place it beneath the eye-to-shoulder skin fold, restore the former outer ring as granular cheek skin, and preserve the frog, limb and toe anatomy, scene content, composition and evidence boundaries. Frames 02, 04 and 05 needed a second pass to remove the remaining oversized outer ring. No correction pass introduced prey, eggs, handling, contact, sex traits, text or a second animal.
